# Research abstract

We investigate astrophysically motivated, unsupervised anomaly ranking alongside Box Least Squares (BLS) transit searches in public TESS photometry. Physics-informed denotes feature engineering, not physical equations enforced during training. Quality filtering, normalization, segment-local detrending and BLS searches yield variability, transit morphology and event-coherence features. Catalogue-dependent stellar proxies are excluded from default model inputs. Following descriptive development on 996 targets, models were fitted without labels on 796 targets and score calibration used 200 separate development targets. The compact feature hypothesis was informed by earlier labelled development results; transformations, models and ranking formula were frozen before independent evaluation. On 924 target-disjoint evaluation targets, including 300 catalogue-positive hosts, the compact hybrid recovered 28/300 hosts at an inspection budget of 100, compared with 33/300 for BLS. The paired target-bootstrap interval for the difference of -5 hosts was [-13, 3], conditional on the frozen models. Single-sector and fixed-allocation complementary rankings also did not exceed BLS recovery at this budget. Injection experiments characterize a detrending tradeoff, while diagnostic plots describe associations rather than causal attribution or planetary validation. The experiment demonstrates a reproducible independent evaluation but does not demonstrate improved catalogue-host recovery or reduced inspection workload.
